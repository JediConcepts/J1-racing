## Build order, revised (23 Nov 2026)

Jamie, on seeing the first build plan: "ideally i wanted to see the graphics
and how that would make a difference as thats the visual effect of this -
backend is kind of secondary".

So the picture comes first and playability second. The content of both
phases stands. Their order, and a few things that depended on the order,
change as follows.

1. Stage one is the picture (the section headed "Phase 2" below). Stage two
   is playability (the section headed "Phase 1"). The section headings keep
   their old numbers so references still resolve.
2. The picture stage does not touch the simulation. `SIM_VERSION` stays
   `sim-2`, lap times do not move, the leaderboards are untouched and there
   is no database change. A release of the picture stage is therefore a pure
   visual release. The one sim bump, the new boards and migration 0017 all
   stay with playability.
3. Something to look at comes early. The picture stage is planned in two
   parts. Part one ends with a "look demo": Ultra at one circuit with
   shadows across the whole view, the new sky and time of day, shown to
   Jamie as before and after stills. Part two (clouds, rain, wet road,
   effects, car and world detail, the Auto governor, release) is planned
   after Jamie has seen it. Ultra is a setting, so it must also run without
   error at the other three circuits. Monaco's own polish (the tunnel's
   lighting, the sea, its shadow casters) is part two.
4. Only the plumbing the picture needs goes in front of it:
   - Baked track data (1.1, first bullet), because a newer three.js could
     move the track samples and change every lap.
   - A parity test that runs today's race loop headless and compares a
     digest of the final state against committed values. It is the proof
     that the engine move did not change a lap time. It replaces "phase 1
     golden traces must be unchanged" in 2.6, which assumed the other order.
5. Rain in the picture stage is a look, not a condition. It is an Ultra
   setting, clearly labelled as visual only, with dry grip and the normal
   board. It becomes a race condition with wet grip and its own boards when
   playability lands (1.9 and 1.10), at which point the simple rain look for
   Retro and Modern (1.9) is also added. Goal 6 is met at the end of stage
   two, not stage one.
6. Effects that the design drives from the tyre slip value (smoke, 2.5) use
   the existing `drift` value until the slip value exists.
7. The Auto setting and the governor (2.2) stay in the picture stage, in
   part two. In part one Ultra is a manual choice.
8. Rollout for the picture stage is: branch, tests, preview on the test
   site, Jamie looks (Mac and a real phone), merge, confirm the build
   marker. No database step. Rollout for playability is as written under
   "Rollout", steps 1 to 7.

What this costs, stated plainly: the engine move is the riskiest step and it
now comes first, so nothing ships until it is done; and the driving stays as
it is today until stage two.

## Intent

Jamie asked whether the game's design and playability can be improved using
the latest engines and the best graphics, shadows and weather possible today.

The case study says it plainly: in the five days after the first playable
build, "the game itself changed surprisingly little". Everything around the
game was hardened; the game was not. v2 is the pass the game never had: first
how it drives and races, then how it looks.

Jamie chose to upgrade MCL-64 in place, on a branch, over building a new game
beside it.

Assumed, not stated by Jamie: this may become a second chapter of the case
study, so the work should keep the repository's habits (claims that can be
reproduced, mistakes written down, nothing asserted on trust).

## What exists today (measured, with sources)

Reference: `G` = src/40-game.js, `C` = src/30-cars.js, `T` = src/20-track.js,
`K` = src/10-core.js, `H` = src/index.template.html. The line numbers were
written against the stale copy (18bb12f) and are not renumbered: find the
code by function name.

Circuits
- Four: Monaco GP (`monaco-gp-v1`, first in `TRACKS`), Silverstone
  (`silverstone-v1`), Indianapolis (`indianapolis-v1`) and Brands Hatch
  (`brands-hatch-v1`). Monaco came with `main`. It is narrow (half width
  5.5 m, runoff 4.5 m, so the walls are 10 m from the centreline) and has a
  tunnel, a sea, yachts and street buildings.
- The default saved circuit is still Silverstone.

Driving and racing
- The car never reaches its intended pace. `V_MAX` is 92 m/s (331 km/h) but
  drag caps real top speed at 52.7 m/s (190 km/h), or 60.1 with full boost.
- AI skill barely matters. Because the target speed sits above what the car
  can reach, skill 0.94 and 0.976 differ by 0.2 s a lap at Silverstone.
- The AI leaves the track every lap at Silverstone (Village, The Loop,
  Brooklands, Luffield) and at Brands Hatch (Druids, Surtees).
- At Monaco all six cars, the AI included, reach the barrier in four places
  in a race (the Fairmont hairpin, the Nouvelle Chicane, the Swimming Pool,
  La Rascasse). Measured in a scratch copy when the merge was reviewed, not
  run again for this document. Nothing "fixes" this in the picture stage: it
  would change Monaco's parity digest.
- Grip is one lateral cap (`latCapacity`, T:424) with three surface values:
  road 1.0, kerb 0.88, off track 0.60. There is no slip angle and no tyre
  state, so the limit arrives without warning.
- No slipstream. The player must hold a key for DRS boost; the AI gets it
  automatically.
- No gamepad support. No difficulty setting. No ghost car or time trial.
- Engine sound is off by default (G:1083).
- A paused race cannot be reproduced from its trace: the sim clock advances
  before the pause check (G:1586 against G:1589) and the AI reads that clock.
- The replay trace is recorded (4 bytes a tick) but never stored, never
  uploaded and never played back. `traceTick` (G:1349) has no caller.

Picture
- three.js r128 (2021), WebGL only, vendored as one UMD file.
- "Retro" and "Modern" differ in four things only: target size, tone mapping,
  shadow map on or off, and one post shader flag (G:2477 to 2502).
- The renderer is created with `antialias: true` and the full device pixel
  ratio (`buildRenderer`). The scene is drawn into a render target and only
  the final quad goes to the canvas, so the canvas is the render target
  times that ratio each way: in Modern at ratio 2, in the bench viewport,
  a 6144 x 3386 canvas for a 3072 x 1693 render target. The 5,200,000 pixel
  cap in `resize` limits the render target only.
- One 2048 shadow map covering 70 m around the player, in Modern only. Cars
  cast (chassis, wheels, and the halo when it is shown) and the trees cast
  on every circuit. At Monaco the tunnel, the hotel, the casino, the yachts
  and the street buildings cast as well: roughly 650 casters, counted by a
  reader of the merge and not counted again here. The track ribbons, the
  trees and the ground plane receive, and at Monaco so do the sea and the
  buildings. Stands, the gantry and signs cast nothing.
- The halo is its own mesh on every car, hidden to start. The H key shows
  and hides it.
- The sky is a vertex coloured dome with no sun disc and 14 flat cloud planes.
  A 4000 x 4000 grey ground plane at y = -10 lies under every circuit.
- No weather, no particles, no skid marks, no brake or rain lights.
- No frame rate measurement and no adaptive quality. Phones default to Modern.
- The scanline and glow overlays (H:115 to 137) sit over Modern too.

HUD bug seen at 1728 x 1028
- GEAR is drawn at `y = H-4` with a 7 unit glyph, so three rows fall below
  the canvas (G:2085, G:2088).
- The speed readout overhangs the right edge by one unit because the text
  shadow offset equals the scale (G:2087, K:122).
- The 2600 x 1600 backing caps are not fed back into the HUD unit, so on a
  large high density window the HUD is a third too big and stretched 10%
  sideways (G:959 to 968).
- `resize` has no ResizeObserver, so the canvas can hold a stale size.
- Confidence: all three faults are real by arithmetic. Whether they are the
  whole symptom is to be confirmed in the running game.

Leaderboard
- Boards are partitioned by `track_version` and ranked by race time.
  `sim_version` is stored but not checked, not in the unique index and not in
  the view. A `SIM_VERSION` bump alone would mix old and new times.
- Latest migration is 0016, which seeds Monaco's row. Whether it has been
  applied to the live database is not known from the repository.
  test/migrations.sh asserts exactly four `track_versions` rows.
- `trackById` falls back to the first circuit, which is now Monaco, for an
  unknown id (T:250), which would mislabel a new board. `submitScore` and
  `leaderboard` in src/36-cloud.js also default to `'monaco-gp-v1'`.
- src/36-cloud.js has a second path, to `/api/leaderboard`, taken when the
  cloud client is not enabled. Nothing reaches it today: `postPending`
  checks first, and the board button is only shown once the cloud client
  has started. Nothing in this repository deploys a server for it.

Tests and build outputs
- Five suites run in CI on this branch: maths, physics (with the track bake
  check), parity, music and migrations. `main` has four; parity came with
  this work. test/csp.js is local.
- `node build.js` needs no install. It writes `dist/index.html` (1,322,041
  bytes at 1f2fee9) and `dist/artifact.html`. On `main` both carry the cloud

- The repository has a `package.json`, lockfiles, `server.js` and `src/db`
  that came with the Monaco export. The build uses none of them.

Engine facts checked on 23 Nov 2026
- Current three.js is 0.187.0. It ships ES modules only: there is no UMD
  build. The WebGL build is `three.core.js` plus `three.module.js`; the WebGPU
  build is `three.core.js` plus `three.webgpu.js`.
- `WebGPURenderer` has a WebGL 2 fallback (`forceWebGL`), a node based post
  processing pipeline, and a cascaded shadow addon (`CSMShadowNode`). The
  classic `CSM`, `Sky` and `EffectComposer` addons also still ship.
- The site CSP allows inline scripts and blob URLs, and blocks eval,
  WebAssembly and every external host except the Supabase project.

## Goals

1. The car is rewarding to drive at the limit: the driver can feel and hear
   grip going away and can catch a slide.
2. The race is a race: opponents stay on the track, difficulty levels are
   meaningfully different, and following a car closely pays off.
3. A player with a gamepad can pick it up and play, with rumble.
4. There is a reason to come back alone: time trial against your own ghost.
5. The picture has a top tier that is a clear step beyond Modern: shadows
   across the whole view, time of day, real weather, a wet road.
6. Rain changes the driving, not only the picture.
7. Every claim above is backed by a test or a measurement kept in the repo.

## Out of scope

- A server side validator for lap times. It stays outstanding. This work
  removes two of its blockers (baked track data, a sim that runs under node)
  and claims nothing more.
- Multiplayer, new circuits, pit stops, tyre wear, fuel, damage.
- Authored models, downloaded textures or sampled audio. Everything stays
  generated in code.
- Night racing, weather that changes during a race, a drying line, standing
  water that causes aquaplaning.
- An online time trial board.
- Photorealism.

## What would make this a failure even if it works

- Retro stops looking like Retro. It is the identity of the game.
- Lap times differ by graphics tier, frame rate or browser engine.
- A phone that plays today cannot play v2.
- The page fetches anything from another host, or needs eval or WebAssembly.
- The page grows past 2.5 MB.
- A leaderboard shows times from two different simulations together.
- A README claim becomes untrue and nobody updates it.

## Constraints

- Deterministic sim. Only `dsin`, `dcos`, `dtan`, `datan`, `datan2`, `sqrt`
  and arithmetic may be added to the step. `Math.exp` and `Math.pow` are
  measured identical across V8 and JavaScriptCore but not guaranteed: no new
  uses in the step, and existing uses are covered by the golden trace test
  described below.
- The repo's sign rule stands: change one sign per commit and measure the
  result in the running game.
- `node build.js` keeps working with no install step, because the deploy
  workflow runs it that way. Nothing is installed into the repo; the
  `package.json`, lockfiles, `server.js` and `src/db` that came with the
  Monaco export are left exactly as main has them; tools find Playwright
  and the bundler through the scratchpad.
- `dist/` stays committed. A push to `main` deploys. Push, merge, deploy and
  any change to the live database are gated on Jamie's GO.
- No tool trailers on commits.
- All prose in the repo and in reports follows Jamie's writing rules.

## Approaches considered

A. Upgrade in place, two phases on one branch. Chosen by Jamie.
   Playability first on the current engine, then the engine move and the new
   graphics tier. Keeps the leaderboard, the security work and a direct
   before and after. Carries the old code and its constraints.

B. New game beside it. Highest graphics ceiling, modern toolchain. Loses the
   before and after story and restarts the release and security work.

C. Playability only. Cheapest. Does not answer the graphics, shadows and
   weather half of the request.

Within A, three further choices, all my calls for Jamie to correct:

- One simulation bump, not several. Every change that alters lap times lands
  in `sim-3` together, in phase 1, including wet races. Rain is therefore
  playable in phase 1 with a simple look, so its grip values are set by
  people driving it before they are frozen. Boards reset once and the
  database changes once.
- One renderer for all three tiers, chosen by a spike with pass criteria
  (see phase 2). Two renderers side by side would double the page weight.
- three.js is bundled once into a vendored file by a script, so the everyday
  build stays free of dependencies.

## Phase 1: playability (three.js r128, sim-3)

### 1.1 Make the sim run without a browser

- Bake each circuit's sampled centreline to data. A script samples the four
  circuits with r128 exactly as `Track` does today (T:303 to 305) and writes
  the arrays to a source file using round trip safe number text. `Track`
  reads the baked data. A test proves the baked arrays equal a fresh r128
  sampling. This must land before the engine move, because a newer
  `CatmullRomCurve3` could move the samples and silently change every lap.
- Move race rules out of the DOM layer into a sim core with no `document`
  and no `THREE`: tick, lap and sector logic, contacts, positions, finish,
  grid placement, AI seeds. `Game` drives it and draws it.
- Add a race configuration object: circuit, laps, grid, difficulty,
  conditions (dry or wet), mode (race or time trial), seed. It replaces the
  module globals `HALF_W`, `RUNOFF` and `WALL_HALF` where the sim reads them.
- Fix the pause hazard: a paused tick must not advance the sim clock.
- Give the trace a header: sim version, circuit, laps, grid, difficulty,
  conditions, mode, seed. Lift the silent 12 minute stop into a visible
  "too long to record" state.

File layout is the builder's call. The constraint is that the sim core loads
under node with no stubs.

### 1.2 Performance envelope

- Retune power, drag and gearing so the car reaches 310 to 330 km/h at the
  end of the longest straight on Silverstone, as the `V_MAX` comment always
  intended. Braking distances and the speed profile follow.
- Target: a clean lap of Silverstone by the middle difficulty AI in 88 to
  96 s. Today it is 105 s.
- Camera field of view, shake and audio pitch are retuned to the new range.

### 1.3 Tyre model

- Replace the hard lateral cap with a slip curve: grip builds with slip to a
  peak, then falls away gently so a slide can be held and recovered.
- Braking and throttle share the same grip budget as cornering, so trail
  braking and early throttle both cost lateral grip.
- Surfaces keep their three states, each scaling the curve. A `wetness`
  input from the race configuration scales road grip and scales kerbs and
  painted surfaces harder. Starting values: wet road 0.72 of dry, wet kerb
  0.55. Final values are the builder's, set by play testing.
- The model exposes a slip value per car for sound, rumble and effects.
- The arcade character stays. This is a forgiving curve, not a simulator.

### 1.4 Slipstream

- A car inside a cone behind another, up to about 40 m, has reduced drag,
  strongest when close and aligned. Deterministic, from positions already in
  the step.
- The existing DRS boost stays as it is.

### 1.5 Opponents and difficulty

- The AI speed profile is rebuilt from the same tyre model and the same
  conditions, so the AI brakes for what the car can do.
- Three levels: Rookie, Pro, Legend. Each sets how much of the grip limit the
  AI uses, its braking margin and its boost use. No rubber banding.
- Acceptance: at every level, dry and wet, every AI car completes five solo
  laps of each circuit without leaving the track. Lap time gaps between
  levels are at least 2% each. "Each circuit" now includes Monaco, which is
  much the hardest: its walls are 10 m from the centreline and today's AI
  reaches them in four places. The plan must give Monaco an explicit
  criterion and a stop and report clause.
- Pro is the default. The choice is stored in settings and in the trace.

### 1.6 Input

- Gamepad through the standard mapping: left stick steers, right trigger
  throttle, left trigger brake, face buttons for handbrake, DRS, camera,
  pause and restart. It joins the existing merge point (G:987 to 999) so it
  is damped, quantised and recorded like every other source.
- Rumble where the browser offers it: kerbs, slip, contact, off track.
- Connect and disconnect at any time. A short on screen note names the
  device when it appears.
- Menus must be usable from the pad at least for start, restart and pause.
- Phones: vibration on kerbs and contact where the browser allows it.

### 1.7 Time trial and ghost

- Title screen offers Race or Time Trial.
- Time trial: alone on the circuit, flying laps without limit, sector deltas
  against your best, one key to restart the lap.
- The ghost is your best lap on this device for that circuit, conditions and
  sim version. It is stored as sampled positions and headings, not by
  simulating the lap again, so it survives nothing but a sim bump and needs
  no mid race state capture. Storage is local to the browser.
- The ghost is drawn translucent, never collides and is never in the sim.

### 1.8 Feedback

- Tyre squeal driven by the slip value; kerb and off track surface sounds.
- Engine sound on by default. Music keeps its current default.
- Optional racing line drawn on the road, coloured by whether the car is
  above the profile speed. On by default for Rookie. Render only.
- HUD: fix the three faults above, add a ResizeObserver, show slipstream and
  the time trial delta. The pixel font stays.
- Turn the scanline and glow overlays off outside Retro.

### 1.9 Rain, first version

- The race setup offers Weather: Dry or Rain. This is a race choice, stored
  in the trace, and it is the only weather choice that touches the sim.
- Rain uses the wet grip from 1.3 and the wet AI profile from 1.5.
- The look in this phase is deliberately simple and works on r128 in both
  existing tiers: rain streaks near the camera, a darker road, heavier fog,
  a duller sky, rain and wet tyre sound. It must be obvious at a glance that
  the race is wet.
- Phase 2 replaces this look in Ultra only. Retro and Modern keep it.

### 1.10 Leaderboard

- New board ids for sim-3, one dry and one wet per circuit:
  `silverstone-v2`, `silverstone-v2-wet`, `indianapolis-v2`,
  `indianapolis-v2-wet`, `brands-hatch-v2`, `brands-hatch-v2-wet`, and for
  Monaco `monaco-gp-v2` and `monaco-gp-v2-wet` (needs Jamie's approval,
  because he approved six ids). sim-3 changes the physics on every circuit,
  so without them sim-3 times would land on the `monaco-gp-v1` board, which
  is the mixing this section forbids.
- Migration 0017, the only database change in v2 (0016 is Monaco's seed row
  and must be confirmed live first): insert those eight rows (if Jamie
  approves the Monaco boards; otherwise six boards and ten rows); add a
  `sim_version` column to `track_versions` and make `submit_score` reject a
  mismatch; add a `difficulty` column to scores and expose it in the view.
- The four existing boards stay readable and open, labelled Classic. Their
  times are not deleted and not mixed with new ones.
- Boards rank standard races only: default laps and grid, any difficulty,
  with the difficulty shown beside each time. Time trial does not post.
- The board still states that no time is replay verified.
- Fix the `trackById` fallback so an unknown id is an error, not the first
  circuit (now Monaco), and the two `'monaco-gp-v1'` defaults in
  src/36-cloud.js with it.
- test/migrations.sh is updated for the new row count (twelve, from four,
  if Jamie approves the Monaco boards; otherwise six boards and ten rows)
  and the new checks.
- The migration is additive. The live v1 page must keep working against the
  migrated database, and test/migrations.sh proves that with the calls the
  v1 client makes.

### 1.11 The two build outputs

- `dist/index.html` is the product and gets everything.
- `dist/artifact.html` stays a supported output. It must still build, stay
  under the size cap and boot. Where its host blocks a feature (gamepad,
  vibration, browser storage, WebGPU), the feature turns itself off quietly:
  the ghost lives in memory for the session, and the renderer uses its WebGL
  path. It is not part of the device checks beyond booting.
- On `main` the artifact now carries the cloud layer (build.js renders both
  outputs with it), and five build checks on the fragment were replaced
  with `// removed`. What is left for the fragment is: no doctype, no
  `<body>`, and the three.js notice. v2 does not rely on the removed checks
  and does not restore them. Whether the removal was meant is an open
  question for Jamie (see the calls below).

### 1.12 Tests for phase 1

- Golden trace: a fixed input script on each circuit, dry and wet (eight
  races with Monaco, whose boards need Jamie's approval; six without), run
  under node and under bun, must produce the same digest of final sim state
  on both, and the digest is committed. A control shows the digest changes
  when one input byte changes.
- Tier independence: the same trace produces the same digest whether or not a
  renderer is attached.
- Tyre model properties: grip never exceeds its peak, is symmetric left and
  right, and falls monotonically with wetness.
- AI acceptance from 1.5, run headless.
- Track baking equality from 1.1.
- These join the existing suites in CI: maths, physics, music and migrations
  on `main`, and the parity suite this branch added.

## Phase 2: picture (current three.js)

### 2.1 Engine move

- A script bundles three.js and the addons in use into one vendored file
  that defines the global `THREE`, with the licence notice. It needs a
  bundler and is run by hand when the version changes. `node build.js` only
  reads the vendored file, as today.

### 2.2 Three tiers and a governor

- Tiers: Retro, Modern, Ultra. The setting gains Auto, which is the default.
- Auto picks Ultra on a capable desktop and Modern on phones, then watches
  frame time: it lowers render resolution first, and only then drops a tier,
  saying so on screen. A manual choice is never overridden.
- Weather belongs to the race; tiers belong to the picture. A wet race reads
  as wet in every tier: Retro and Modern keep the simple rain look from
  phase 1, and Ultra gets everything in 2.4.
- Sky settings exist only in Ultra and are shown only when Ultra is active:
  time of day, and cloud cover in the dry (Clear or Overcast). They never
  touch the sim. Retro and Modern keep the single dry look they have today.

### 2.3 Light, shadow and sky (Ultra)

- Cascaded shadows covering the visible track, with cars, trees, stands,
  gantry and signs all casting and all surfaces receiving.
- A physically based sky with a sun whose position comes from the chosen
  time of day: Morning, Midday, Golden hour, Dusk. The environment map and
  fog colour are rebuilt from the sky when a setting changes.
- Layered procedural clouds lit by the sun, and cloud shadows drifting over
  the ground.
- Post processing: bloom, a light motion blur tied to speed, colour grading
  per time of day, antialiasing.
- At Dusk: car rain lights and brake lights glow, and the stands and gantry
  carry emissive lighting. This is low sun, not night.

### 2.4 Rain (Ultra)

- This replaces the simple rain look from phase 1 in Ultra. The sim, the
  grip values and the boards do not change.
- Rain streaks around the camera that respect wind and car speed; splashes
  on the road; spray plumes behind cars that grow with speed and hide the
  car ahead; droplets on the lens in the onboard cameras.
- Wet road: darker, smoother, with puddles from a noise mask, reflecting the
  sky, the cars and their lights.
- Flashing red rain light on every car in the wet.
- Rain and wet tyre sound.

### 2.5 World and car detail (Ultra)

- Road surface detail generated in code: grain, the rubbered line, painted
  kerbs and grid slots.
- Trees with volume, instanced, swaying in the wind. Fuller stands.
- Car: clear coat paint, emissive brake and rain lights, glowing brake discs
  under heavy braking, wheel blur at speed.
- Effects in all tiers at suitable cost: tyre smoke from the slip value,
  grass and gravel dust, wall sparks, skid marks.
- Instancing where it pays, so Indianapolis with 33 cars stays within budget.
  Monaco is now the heavy scene: every street building and every yacht part
  is its own mesh and casts a shadow, so merging or limiting them belongs
  here too.

## Appendix: r128 behaviours the engine move must handle

From reading the source against the current three.js API. Versions are from
memory and are confirmed against the migration guide during planning. The
line numbers are stale (see the base line at the top); the function, or the
line to look for, is named beside each.

- Texture colour space: `sRGBEncoding` and `.encoding` (K:186, G:128, G:185:
  `texFromCanvas`, `buildRenderer`, `buildEnvironment`) became
  `SRGBColorSpace` and `.colorSpace`.
- Double conversion: `convertSRGBToLinear()` (K:172, C:15: `srgb` and `part`)
  would convert twice, because colour management now converts hex input
  itself. Monaco's scenery and the ground plane take their colours through
  `srgb()`, so they are covered when `srgb` is.
- Raw hex colours elsewhere shift the other way: sky (T:1139 to 1141,
  `buildSky`, and `SKY_HAZE`), fog and clear colour (G:113, the
  `setClearColor` call in `buildRenderer`; G:201, the `THREE.Fog` in
  `buildWorld`), lights (G:207 to 213 in `buildWorld`, G:368 `setLights`,
  T:905 in `buildStartGantry`). Monaco added two in
  `buildMonacoTunnelAndUrbanScenery`: `new THREE.Color(0xffd700)`, used as
  a vertex colour on the tunnel's light strips, and the yacht window colour
  `0x050505`.
- Light intensity: the legacy scale was removed. Expect roughly a factor of
  pi on the hemisphere, directional and ambient lights (G:207 to 213, in
  `buildWorld`).
- Tone mapping and output colour space no longer apply when rendering into a
  render target, so the arrangement at G:121 to 128 (`this.rt` in
  `buildRenderer`) stops working. The post pass must tone map and encode.
- WebGL 1 was removed. The boot probe asks for `webgl` (G:2649, the
  `probe.getContext('webgl')` line in the closure at the foot of the file)
  and the post shaders are written in the older GLSL style (G:21 to 55,
  `POST_VERT` and `POST_FRAG`).
- The global `THREE` is assumed in every source file, in the boot check
  (G:2643, the `window.THREE` test in the same closure), in
  test/physics.test.js:50 to 66 (where the vendored file and the source
  chain are read and evaluated) and in test/sim-parity.test.js.
  test/track-bake.test.js and tools/bake-tracks.js stay on r128.
  test/music.test.js loads src/10-core.js with no `THREE` at all, so that
  file must stay a plain script.
- Sky and clouds use `MeshBasicMaterial` and are tone mapped today. So are
  the start banner and lights, the corner boards, the car's blob shadow,
  and at Monaco the tunnel's light strips, its banner, the casino sign and
  the yacht windows.
- The shadow camera needs `updateProjectionMatrix` after its box is edited
  (G:228 to 232, in `buildWorld`).
- The renderer is created with `antialias: true` and the full device pixel
  ratio. The engine move keeps both.
- Expected first symptom of a missed item: a dark, flat frame.

Not affected: geometry class names, the `PMREMGenerator` calls (G:187 to
191, in `buildEnvironment`), and `physicallyCorrectLights`, which is not
used.
