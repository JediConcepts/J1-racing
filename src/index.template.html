FULLSCREEN ON IPHONE

iPhone browsers cannot put a web page fullscreen — Apple allows it for video only, and that applies to Chrome and Firefox on iOS too, since they all run on WebKit. There is one way round it:

  1. Tap Share in the browser toolbar
  2. Choose Add to Home Screen
  3. Launch MCL‑64 from the new icon

Started that way it runs with no address bar, properly fullscreen.

MCL‑64 — Papaya Grand Prix

A Formula 1 racer built the way a 1997 console would have built it: 240p, a five‑bit dither, and no anti‑aliasing anywhere. Three laps of a stylised Silverstone against a full grid, in a browser, with nothing to install.

How to play

On a keyboard, steer with the arrows or A and D, and use the throttle and brake with the up and down arrows or W and S. Space is the handbrake, Shift arms DRS on the two detection zones, and C switches between the chase camera and the cockpit.

On a phone, put a thumb anywhere on the screen and an analogue stick appears under it — left and right to steer, push forward for throttle, pull back to brake and then reverse. Steering by tilting the handset is optional, and the throttle can hold itself open so one thumb is enough.

The circuit

Five and a half kilometres of stylised Silverstone: Copse, Maggotts, Becketts, Hangar Straight, Stowe, Vale and Club, with the kerbs and the two DRS zones roughly where you would expect them. The grid is six cars and the race is three laps, so a full run takes a little over five minutes.

The leaderboard

Race times post to a global board. Signing in is by Google or by a link emailed to you — there is no password to choose or forget. Your driver name is the only thing shown publicly; email addresses and surnames are held in a separate schema the public API cannot reach at all.

How it is built

One HTML file with no dependencies to install, no build step for the player, and no external requests at runtime. The physics is deterministic to the last bit on every browser, which is what makes a submitted lap time verifiable rather than merely plausible: each race records a replay trace that reproduces it exactly.
